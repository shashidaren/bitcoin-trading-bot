## 1. THE FINDING — the edge is smaller than the broker spread

XM's published average BTCUSD spread is **≈ $22.50–60 per 1 BTC**
([fxstreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/brokers/reviews/xm) quotes 22.50;
[gojj](https://gojj.com/review/xm/) quotes $60 ≈ 0.0727% of price). At
`LOT_SIZE = 0.01` a round trip costs **$0.225–0.60 per trade**.

| | gross / trade | cost / trade | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| from 09-05 (n=71) | +$0.165 | $0.225 … $0.60 | −$0.06 … −$0.44 |
| new regime (n=19) | +$0.46 | $0.225 … $0.60 | −$0.14 … +$0.24 |

In risk units at a typical live ATR of $85 (1R = 2×ATR×0.01 = $1.70): the spread
is **0.13–0.35R per trade**. This is why `win_rate_report.py` §8 exists —
breakeven decisive WR = (1 + s/a)/3:

| ATR $ | s=$0.10 | s=$0.25 | s=$0.50 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 50% | 75% | 117% |
| 15 | 44% | 61% | 89% |
| 30 | 39% | 47% | 61% |
| 85 | 35% | 37% | 39% |
| 150 | 34% | 36% | 39% |

The arithmetic rule: to keep the round trip at **≤15% of 1R**,

> **ATR ≥ 333 × spread$**  — i.e. ATR ≥ $73 at a $0.22 spread, ATR ≥ $200 at $0.60.

The live `MIN_ATR_PERCENT = 0.01%` is **$7.70** at $77k. It has never been a cost
control. Two consequences:

1. **Measure the real XM BTCUSD spread before anything else.** It is the single
   number that decides whether the current M5 stream is tradeable at 0.01 lot,
   and it can be read off the MT5 sidecar's tick data (or the symbol spec) in an
   afternoon. Then decide between: a lower-spread account (Ultra Low / Zero),
   a cost-ratio ATR floor as above, or fewer/larger-ATR trades.
2. A cost-ratio floor is **not** a curve-fit. Unlike the win-rate filters in
   §2–§4 it needs no within-day control — it is an identity, and it is the only
   version of "raise `MIN_ATR_PERCENT`" this data supports.

### 1b. Measured Spread & Impact (2026-09-15 live terminal measurement)

XM BTCUSD spread measured 2026-09-15 from the live terminal = **$40.00/BTC** = **$0.40 per round trip at 0.01 lot**.
Symbol `BTCUSD` exists with contract `1.0` / min lot `0.01`, while `BTCUSDm` does not exist (so `SYMBOL_MT5=BTCUSD`).

Netting that cost:
- 71 trades from 09-05 go from **+$11.74 gross** to **−$16.66 net**.
- Live era (n=19) is **+$1.13 net**, but **+$12.62** of that is 09-10 alone (without it **−$10.29 net**).

| Era / Slice | Gross P&L | Net P&L (@ $0.40/trade) | Net / Trade |
|---|---|---|---|
| from 09-05 (n=71) | +$11.74 | **−$16.66** | −$0.235 |
| live era (n=19) | +$8.73 | **+$1.13** | +$0.059 |
| live era excl 09-10 (n=16) | −$3.89 | **−$10.29** | −$0.643 |

**Cause**: M5 ATR is too small vs a $40 spread — live-era median entry ATR $84 makes cost **23.9% of 1R** with a breakeven win rate of **41.3%** vs an actual **36.8%**.

Same data re-cut per timeframe:
- **M5**: 24.0% of 1R (breakeven WR 41.3%)
- **M15**: 11.9% of 1R (breakeven WR 37.3%)
- **H1**: 5.4% of 1R (breakeven WR 35.1%)
- **H4**: 2.6% of 1R (breakeven WR 34.2%)

So the open decision is the **bar timeframe**, not BE/TP parameters, and BE/TP tuning is deferred until then.

near the floor. **Verdict: reject ATR-floor-by-win-rate. Use §1's cost ratio.**

## 5. The BE ratchet: still not adopted, and BTC's value is not gold's 0.75R

Gold's review found its own 0.30R ratchet was armed by noise and raised it to
0.75R. BTC has **no** ratchet, so the honest question is not "copy 0.75R" but
"does a ratchet help BTC, and at what trigger?" Both views, net of $0.25:

**Real trades, 19 log-covered, 240-min horizon (cascade-ignorant):**

| BE trigger | W/L/BE/T | WR | P/L |
|---|---|---|---|
| off (live) | 6/12/0/1 | 33.3% | +$0.14 |
| +0.50R | 5/7/7/0 | 41.7% | +$4.99 |
| +0.75R | 6/10/3/0 | 37.5% | −$1.41 |
| +1.00R | 6/9/4/0 | 40.0% | +$1.19 |
| +1.25R | 5/9/5/0 | 35.7% | +$0.46 |

**Cascade-aware takeable census (78 signals, 1 position + escalating cooldown +
3-SL daily halt), net of $0.25:**

| BE trigger | taken | W/L/BE/T | WR | P/L |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| off (live) | 25 | 8/16/0/1 | 33.3% | +$5.98 |
| +0.50R | 30 | 6/11/12/1 | 35.3% | +$4.02 |
| +0.75R | 30 | 7/14/8/1 | 33.3% | +$1.50 |
| **+1.00R** | 29 | 9/14/5/1 | 39.1% | **+$8.69** |
| +1.25R | 28 | 9/16/2/1 | 36.0% | +$6.72 |

What to take from this:

- The **direction matches gold**: a tight ratchet (0.5–0.75R) loses to no
  ratchet. BTC's own 0.75R row is the *worst* in both views — gold's adopted
  constant is a gold-specific optimum, not a universal one.
- The **magnitude does not clear the bar**: the best row (+1.0R) beats off by
  **$2.71 on 29 cascade trades**, and the two views do not even agree on which
  trigger is best (+0.50R wins on the 19 real trades, +1.00R on the cascade
  census). That is 2–3 trades of noise, not evidence.
- **Verdict: status unchanged from HANDOFF §7(a) — not adopted.** If a live
  trigger is ever armed, use +1.0R, and all tools are already BE-aware so the
  ledger can record a `BE` exit reason. Re-check when the live era has 30+
  trades *and* the real spread is known (a ratchet changes the cost/R balance).

## 7. The 09-11 → 09-14 collapse is regime, not parameters

Nineteen live-era trades, last ten 1W/9L. Day anatomy from the log:

| day | day range | ÷avg ATR | census signals → outcomes | book |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 09-12 | 446 | 15.0× | 21 (19 SELL, 2 BUY) → **21 SL** | 2 trades, 0W/2L |
| 09-13 | 950 | 20.8× | 11 → 10 SL, 1 TP | 3 trades, 0W/3L |
| 09-14 | 3,203 | 26.2× | 16 → 7 BUY TP, 7 BUY SL, 2 SELL SL | 4 trades, 1W/3L |

On 09-12 the engine fired 19 SELL signals into a drifting range with a median
ATR of **$29** — a 2×ATR stop of **$58** against hourly candles whose ranges
ran $52–$234 (median $107). Every one of the 21 signals stopped. On 09-14 the same funnel fired
14 BUY signals into a $3,203 range and went 7 TP / 7 SL; the book caught only 4
of them (1W/3L) because the cooldown/halt clipped the sequence.

So the recent bleeding has a mechanism, and it is the same friction the rest of
this review keeps hitting: **the stop distance is calibrated to the current ATR,
but the noise it must survive is the day's structure — and the cost is set by
the spread.** Both §1 (cost/ATR) and this section (noise/ATR) argue for trading
only when ATR is large relative to frictions, which is a very different (and
defensible) rule from "raise the ATR floor because low-ATR trades won less".

For completeness, "does the day have room for a 2R target at all?" (day range ÷
6×ATR): 09-07 **0.99**, 09-08 3.23, 09-09 3.43, 09-10 3.96, 09-11 5.94,
09-12 2.54, 09-13 4.47, 09-14 4.61, 09-15 **0.98**. On the two thinnest days a
single SL+TP journey barely fits in the whole day.

## 8. Data hygiene

- **The ledger spans two geometries** and every pooled R statistic is invalid
  across them: before 09-06 17:49 it is SL 1.5×ATR / TP 2.5×ATR (RR 1:1.67,
  breakeven WR 37.5%) with candle fills; from 09-06 17:49 it is the live
  2×/4× (1:2, 33.3%). `check_data.py` now censuses both eras, warns on mixed
  geometry, and sanity-checks realized R (±0.25R) on live-era rows only;
  `replay_lib.planned_rr/geometry/realized_r/GEOM2_DEPLOY` are the single
  source of truth.
- **Exits fill on ticks, not levels.** 0 of 36 live-era exits landed on an M5
  boundary. Median SL overshoot is −0.110 ATR (≈5.5% of risk); median TP
  overshoot +0.128 ATR (max +0.520). Two rows (#40 −1.38R, #52 −1.38R) are
  fast-move tick timing — **not** lot/size/schema drift. `analyze_losers.py` §5
  skips pre-09-06 17:49 rows by design for this reason.
- **Costs are not in the ledger at all.** Every P/L in this repo is gross;
  §1 is the missing half.
- Bar census: 2,098 log rows, 0 duplicate minutes, **one missing M5 slot
  (09-14 08:45)**. `check_data.py` now flags this class.
- Two 09-03 outliers (−$394.84, 100× lot) remain in `trades.csv`; they are
  excluded explicitly (never silently) everywhere.
