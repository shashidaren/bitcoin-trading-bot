## 2. Is the edge real?

`python3 tools/live_readiness.py --spread 0.40` (strictly post-gate, n = 66).

### 2.1 Significance
- Net **+$0.470/trade**, sd $3.14, se $0.387, **t = +1.22**; 95 % CI **[−0.30, +1.24]** per trade. The interval comfortably contains zero.
- Bootstrap: the average net trade is positive in **90 %** of iid resamples and **85 %** of resamples that redraw whole calendar days (trades cluster by day: 12 up days averaging +$6.12, 10 down days averaging −$4.24). A pass bar of 90 % by day was not met. (Not a posterior probability; it also ignores regime shifts, so it is optimistic.)
- In R: **+0.33R gross, only +0.06R net** per trade. In dollars the book looks better than in R because the high-ATR trades (low cost share) carry the dollars.
- **Power:** at 22.4 trades/week, ~350 total trades (≈ 13 more weeks) are needed to confirm an edge of the observed size with 80 % power at 5 % two-sided; ~1,400 (≈ 60 more weeks) if the true edge is half of it. *Statistical confirmation cannot be the gate for starting a pilot* — the pilot's job is to validate execution and cost (§7).

### 2.2 Cost margin
- Gross +$0.870/trade ⇒ **break-even round trip = $0.87/trade = $87/BTC**; assumed $0.40 ⇒ cover 2.2×.
- Net P/L (66 trades) by round-trip spread: $0.25 **+40.94** · $0.40 **+31.04** · $0.60 +17.84 · $0.80 +4.64 · $1.00 **−8.56** · $1.50 −41.56.
- Median 1R is now **$1.67** (was $1.79 at 99 trades) → $0.40 is **24 %** of 1R (was 22.3 %) → cost-adjusted break-even WR **41.3 %** vs 43.9 % observed (Wilson 32.6–55.9). Recent entries are lower-ATR, so the cost burden is *rising*. At the worst trade the spread is 87 % of 1R.

### 2.3 Fragility
| Remove the best… | removed | net without them |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 trade | +7.45 (24 %) | +23.59 |
| 3 trades | +19.32 (62 %) | +11.72 |
| **5 trades** | **+30.53 (98 %)** | **+0.51** |
| 10 trades | +53.19 (171 %) | −22.15 |
| the 3 best days (09-21, 09-30, 09-10) | +39.14 (126 %) | −8.10 |

A 2:1 payoff at 44 % hit rate *should* concentrate profit in the winners, so this
is partly structural — but it means ~5 trades decided the result and one of them
(#122, +$5.94) is the +3.05R TP overshoot flagged by `check_data`.

### 2.4 Stability
First 33 post-gate trades: 13W/20L, +$7.12 (+0.22/trade). Last 33: 16W/17L, +$23.92 (+0.72/trade). By ISO week: W37 +3.25 (n = 11) · W38 **+0.36** (n = 21) · W39 +11.80 (n = 25) · W40 +15.63 (n = 9). Max drawdown $15.41 (50 % of final profit), longest losing streak 8, and **28 trades / 9.1 days below the previous equity high** (peak +$26.44 on 09-23, no new high until 09-30/10-01). That is what "positive expectancy" feels like at this scale: long flat stretches.

## 3. Where the edge appears to live (exploratory — hypotheses, not parameters)

This review cut the post-gate sample ~10 ways (ATR, cost share, side, weekday/weekend, hour…).
A Bonferroni bar for that many looks is ≈ 0.005, and one 3-week regime is not
repeated sampling. Treat everything here as a **logging and demo-stage test
plan**, not a retune (HANDOFF §7 "NOT queued" still stands).

| Cut | n | W/L | WR | net | net/trade | note |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| entry ATR < $60 | 13 | 2/11 | 15.4 % | −10.44 | −0.80 | spread = 46 % of 1R; even *gross* −$5.24 |
| entry ATR $60–90 | 25 | 11/14 | 44.0 % | +3.49 | +0.14 | break-even WR 42 % |
| entry ATR $90–130 | 18 | 11/7 | 61.1 % | +27.05 | +1.50 | |
| entry ATR ≥ $130 | 10 | 5/5 | 50.0 % | +10.94 | +1.09 | |
| **spread ≤ 20 % of 1R** | 24 | 13/11 | 54.2 % | +30.33 | +1.26 | |
| spread 20–30 % of 1R | 27 | 13/14 | 48.1 % | +10.68 | +0.40 | |
| spread 30–40 % of 1R | 5 | 2/3 | 40.0 % | −0.73 | −0.15 | |
| spread > 40 % of 1R | 10 | **1/9** | 10.0 % | −9.24 | −0.92 | |
| BUY | 32 | 16/16 | 50.0 % | **+31.36** | +0.98 | |
| SELL | 34 | 13/21 | 38.2 % | **−0.32** | −0.01 | no net edge so far |
| weekday entries | 50 | 26/24 | 52.0 % | +43.28 | +0.87 | |
| **weekend entries** | 16 | **3/13** | 18.8 % | −12.24 | −0.76 | Fisher p = 0.023; all 3 weekends negative |

1. **Low-volatility trades lose** — monotonically as the cost share rises
   (54 % → 48 % → 40 % → 10 % WR). This is consistent with the cost identity
   (break-even WR = (1 + spread/1R)/3) and is the first-principles version of a
   filter the handoff rightly rejected when it was a *win-rate* split. The
   live-spread guard the handoff already requires (it named it a LIVE blocker)
   should be defined **as a share of 1R**, not only in dollars. In-sample it would have blocked 15 trades
   (−$9.97 net) at X = 0.30. `MIN_ATR_PERCENT` (0.01 % ≈ $8) is far below any
   cost-sensible floor, as the handoff already notes.
2. **SELL has contributed nothing net of cost** (34 trades, −$0.32). BUY carries
   the result — in a window where BTC rose 5.4 % and EMA50 > EMA200 for much of
   it. Don't disable SELL on 34 trades; do watch it in demo.
3. **Weekends are 3W/13L** across three independent weekends, and third-party
   guides put XM's weekend spreads at 2–4× the weekday norm (§5.1). Plausible mechanism,
   weak statistics. Cheapest test: trade weekends on **demo** (free data), pause
   them in the **micro-live** phase until that data is in.

## 4. What a live account would experience (0.01 lot, $200 base)

Bootstrap of the 66 net trades, 100 trades ≈ 31 days (6,000 paths each):

| Scenario | P(net < 0) | 5th pct | median | 95th pct | median max-DD | 95th max-DD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| edge as observed (+$0.47/trade) | 6 % | −4.2 | +47.4 | +98.7 | 19.6 | 38.8 |
| **zero edge**, same dispersion | **51 %** | −51.6 | −1.1 | +50.9 | 33.2 | 68.0 |
| spread doubles (+$0.40/trade) | 41 % | −44.7 | +6.7 | +60.1 | 30.0 | 61.2 |

**A month of live P/L cannot tell "real edge" from "no edge"** — the zero-edge
path ends negative half the time and shows $33 of drawdown at the median. What a
month *can* tell you is execution quality (fills, spread, slippage, missed
exits) — hence the pilot's purpose.

How informative is a kill-switch floor? P(equity touches −$X at any point in 100 trades):

| Scenario | −$20 | **−$30** | −$40 | −$50 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| edge as observed | 11 % | **4 %** | 1 % | 0 % |
| zero edge | 50 % | **32 %** | 19 % | 10 % |
| spread doubles | 41 % | **24 %** | 13 % | 7 % |

−$30 (≈ 17R; 15 % of $200) rarely fires if the edge is as observed (4 %) and
fires about one path in three if it is not — a defensible pilot hard-stop.

## 5. What the paper results do not cover

### 5.1 Spread and cost
The only XM measurement is **$40/BTC at 04:06 UTC on 09-15 (Asian session)**
(PROJECT_LOG). Third-party XM guides — *unverified, not XM-published* — put
typical BTCUSD spreads at $30–80, **$100–300 in high volatility and $100–200 at
weekends** ([xmsignal.com guide](https://xmsignal.com/en/blog/xm-crypto-trading-guide/)).
Against an $87 break-even those weekend figures are loss-making before any
slippage, and the weekend slice is already −$12.24 at the *Asian* spread. XM lists
crypto CFDs as 24/7 with possible short weekend maintenance; sources disagree on
whether crypto carries swap, so **read the Specification tab** (swap, commission,
stops level, execution mode, filling modes) before trusting a $0.40 model.
Leverage/margin is *not* a constraint at 0.01 lot (≈ $840 notional).

### 5.2 Feed
The paper fills come from a ~1 tick/second spot-style stream (tick volume a steady
≈ 240–280 per bar all month, two-decimal prices, no feed switch in the log) — i.e.
the Twelve Data path; the handoff does not record which `DATA_SOURCE` the box
runs, so confirm. A live order triggers on **XM's own CFD quotes**, which have
their own basis, spread and tick cadence. The signal is a wick rejection at a
20-bar extreme, so which venue prints the high/low matters: signal parity
between the two feeds is unproven. `DATA_SOURCE=MT5` already exists to run the
*forward test* on XM candles at zero risk.

### 5.3 Entry latency — not a problem
The simulator fills at the signal-bar close; a live order cannot. Re-walking the
66 post-gate trades on the M5 log with the fill 1, 2.5 and 5 minutes late (SL/TP
distances re-based on the delayed fill, as the live order would) gives net
**+$17.14 (0) / +$18.66 (1 min) / +$17.79 (2.5 min) / +$20.39 (5 min)** — flat
within noise. (Coarse: M5 OHLC, stop-first ties, 240-min horizon; read the rows
against each other, not against the ledger's +$31.04.) The live loop polls every
10 s, so this risk is small.

### 5.4 Operations
`forward_test_log.csv` is missing **183 of 6,694 expected M5 slots (97.3 % present;
`check_data.py`'s count)**, dominated by one **885-minute (14.8 h) outage
09-28 17:50 → 09-29 08:35**. No
trade straddled it, and a live position would have kept its server-side SL/TP —
but the bookkeeping, the daily-loss breaker (it reads `trades.csv`) and the
stale-feed alert path were all blind for 15 hours, and the cause is not recorded
in the repo (check `journalctl` on the box). The handoff's "no gap > 15 min" is
no longer true. For live money a dead feed needs a *position-aware* alarm.

### 5.5 One regime
The whole sample is 3.3 weeks in which BTC ranged $74,971–$87,396 (+5.4 % net).
No crash, no violent trend, no volatility regime change. The decisive missing
evidence is **the same frozen rules on a multi-month, multi-regime history**
(bull/bear/chop, M5 vs M15/H1, percentage-based costs). It could not be run in
this session: the sandbox has no outbound network, and the repo carries no older
M5 data.

**Missing safeguards** (not found anywhere in `engine.py`): equity/drawdown kill
switch (only 3 SLs/day), demo-vs-real account check (`account_info().trade_mode`),
`terminal_info().trade_allowed` / `account_info().trade_allowed`, stale-quote
check on the tick time, market-closed/maintenance retcode handling, a manual-halt
file, and a position-aware dead-man alert.

**Design note.** `TRADING_MODE` is a *code constant* (`engine.py` L19) and
autosync deploys `origin/main` to `/opt/bitcoin` — so a demo/live run configured
by editing the constant on the box is fragile. Make it an env var (default
`FORWARD_TEST`) with an explicit `MT5_ACCOUNT_KIND=demo|real` guard.

## 7. Path to live — stages and exit criteria

Thresholds are **proposals** for you to accept or change; nothing here is enabled.
`TRADING_MODE` stays `FORWARD_TEST` and merging any of this does **not** switch
LIVE on.

**Stage A — make the order path correct (engineering, no money)**
- Fix findings 1–7 and 9 (§6); design the Wine order-executor (finding 4).
- Add the spread guard (share of 1R **and** hard $ cap, e.g. ≤ $60/BTC ≈ 70 % of the $87 break-even until the measured profile says otherwise).
- Kill switches: equity floor (§4: −$30 at 0.01 lot), existing 3-SL daily halt, one position per magic, `trade_allowed`/demo-vs-real check at start, manual halt file, stale-quote check, position-aware dead-man alert.
- **Exit:** `live_path_probe.py` exits 0; the probes are promoted to smoke scenarios; smoke test passes.

**Stage B — measure (zero risk; start today, in parallel with A)**
1. **Spread logger** in the Wine sidecar: XM bid/ask every minute for ≥ 14 days incl. two weekends and the blackout windows → hour × weekday distribution → a session-aware `--spread`, and the weekend rule.
2. **Feed parity:** run the forward test on `DATA_SOURCE=MT5` for ≥ 14 days (record it as a new era boundary in HANDOFF §9) and compare signal flow and entries with the Twelve Data era.
3. **Multi-regime offline backtest** of the frozen rules on public BTC history (needs network): M5 vs M15/H1, %-based costs. This also settles the handoff's "timeframe decision" better than waiting for more 3-week samples.
4. **Prospective logging, no gating:** spread-to-1R, weekend flag, side — on every signal and skip row. Re-read at ≥ 100 post-gate trades.

**Stage C — XM demo account through the real order path**
- Trade **everything, including weekends** (free data). No rule changes during the run.
- **Exit (proposed):** ≥ 30 closed demo trades **and** ≥ 2 weekends (≈ 3–4 weeks); ≥ 90 % of the trades the simulator would have taken are matched by a demo fill (or a logged guard skip with its reason); every fill ↔ ledger row to the cent (price, profit + commission + swap) with **0 orphaned positions and 0 missed exits** across ≥ 2 deliberate mid-position restarts and one induced feed outage; median realised all-in round trip (spread + slippage + commission + swap) **≤ $0.60/trade** and no session/weekend bucket whose median exceeds $1.00.

**Stage D — micro-live pilot (only if A–C pass)**
- 0.01 lot (the simulator's size and the broker minimum), one position at a time, funded only with money you can lose entirely.
- **Hard stop at −$30 net from pilot start** (stop and review — never average down); daily 3-SL halt on; spread guard on; **weekdays only** unless demo says otherwise; run ≥ 50 closed trades (~3 weeks).
- Purpose: confirm real fills and costs, **not** prove profit (that takes ~350 trades; expect ≈ +$10/week ± $15 if the edge is real).
- **Continue if:** expectancy after actual costs is not worse than demo's, 0 reconciliation breaks, floor not touched.

**Stage E — scale rule:** no size increase until ≥ 100 closed live trades **and** the lower 80 % bound of live net/trade (after actual costs) is > 0 (at n = 100, sd ≈ $3.1 that needs ≈ +$0.40/trade observed). Then step up gradually and recompute §4 at the new size.

**Earliest sensible timeline:** ~1–2 weeks for A, B runs alongside, 3–4 weeks for C → a micro-live pilot **~5–6 weeks from now at the soonest.** The one place it could be compressed is C's duration — not below 30 trades and two weekends — and this review does not recommend it.

**Data gates today** (`tools/live_readiness.py`, thresholds are constants at the top of the file): **3/6 pass** — FAIL sample (66 < 100), FAIL edge (85 % < 90 %), PASS cost cover (2.2× ≥ 2.0×), PASS fragility (+$5.87 without the best 4), PASS recent form (+$0.78 over the last 25), **FAIL feed (885-min gap in the last 14 days)**. Three of the passes are marginal; re-run weekly. Passing every data gate is *necessary, not sufficient*.

## 8. Caveats — what I did not verify

- **The probe's MT5 is a fake** built from the documented semantics. It proves the engine's logic fails *under those semantics*; XM's behaviour (filling modes, execution mode, retcodes, maintenance windows) is broker-specific — the demo run is the real test.
- **Spread:** the only measured value is one sample; third-party figures are unverified. Swap/commission treatment is unconfirmed.
- **Feed:** "Twelve Data" is inferred from tick cadence and price format; `DATA_SOURCE` on the box isn't in the repo. The 885-min outage's cause is unknown.
- **Statistics:** bootstrap/day-block resampling assume exchangeability and ignore regime shifts; the CIs are optimistic. Section 3 cuts are post-hoc on one regime; the weekend Fisher p (0.023) does not survive a multiple-comparison correction. Gate thresholds are proposals.
- **No multi-regime backtest** (no network in the sandbox) and no M15/H1 replay — the handoff's primary blocker remains open.
- **No engine, strategy or parameter was changed**, `TRADING_MODE` was not touched, and nothing was merged.
